3 Tips to Help Your High Schooler Ace the SAT

As an SAT tutor, I’ve found that the strongest and most effective SAT preparation comes from identifying the skills a student needs most, building steady study habits, and practicing under conditions that resemble the real test.

For homeschool families, SAT prep can be especially personalized. Your flexible schedule may let your high schooler spend more time on challenging skills, move quickly through familiar material, and build a testing plan around their educational objectives and chosen test date. Here are three strategies I recommend to help your student prepare with confidence.

Start With a Diagnostic and Expert Support

Have your student complete a full-length diagnostic practice test so you can establish a baseline before spending too much time in. The goal isn’t simply to get an initial score. It is to understand where and why points are being lost.

A score report may reveal that your student needs more practice with advanced math, grammar conventions, data analysis, or reading comprehension. It can also show test-taking habits that affect performance. A common pattern I’ve seen is that students may understand algebra yet make avoidable calculation errors under pressure. Another may know grammar rules but miss questions because they read too quickly or overlook a key word in the prompt.

An effective SAT plan addresses both content knowledge and test execution.

Working with an experienced SAT tutor can make this process more efficient. A tutor can review diagnostic results, spot patterns in missed questions, and create a realistic plan based on the student’s starting point, target score, and testing timeline. One-on-one instruction also gives students dedicated time to ask questions, work through difficult concepts, and stay accountable between sessions.

Through Wyzant, families can connect with SAT tutors who offer personal support based on a student’s needs. Parents and students can review tutor credentials, read reviews, and find an instructor whose experience, teaching style, and availability fit.

Test-prep tip: Don’t just look at your tutor’s SAT score. A great tutor is patient and can explain concepts plainly while also teaching students how to make smart decisions under time pressure.

Build Accuracy First, Then Add Timing

The digital SAT includes two sections: Reading and Writing, and Math. Students have 64 minutes for 54 Reading and Writing questions and 70 minutes for 44 Math questions. Each section is divided into two modules, so students must manage both their pacing and their academic skills.

One of the most common mistakes I see is introducing strict timing too early. Before students worry about speed, they need to learn how to solve questions accurately. If a student is still unsure about a grammar rule or math concept, rushing through more questions can reinforce the habits that lead to missed points.

Start with untimed practice. Depending on what your student struggles with, give them time to learn the underlying skill and apply it correctly. Once accuracy becomes more consistent, begin adding timed sets.

A weekly SAT routine might include:

  • Two short instruction sessions focused on priority skills.
  • One timed Reading and Writing module.
  • One timed Math module.
  • One revision session for missed or uncertain questions
  • A full-length practice test every few weeks
  • The revision session is where much of the improvement happens. Don’t just check the answer key; encourage your student to label why they missed each question.

For instance, missing a question because they did not recognize a grammar rule calls for content review. Missing it because they overlooked the word “except” is a reading-precision issue. Knowing the difference helps students use their study time more strategically.

Test-prep tip: After reviewing a missed question, students should be able to explain why the correct answer works, why their original choice was tempting, and what they will do differently next time.

Practice the Digital Test Experience

Knowing the content is essential, but SAT success also requires comfort with the testing format. The SAT is administered digitally through College Board’s Bluebook application and uses a multistage adaptive design: performance in the first module helps determine the difficulty of the second module.

Students should focus on answering each question thoroughly, using their time strategically, and remaining calm when later questions feel more challenging.

Full-length digital practice tests are one of the best ways to build those skills. They help students develop endurance, adjust to screen-based reading, and experience the pace of the full exam. Treat a practice test as a rehearsal.

Try to recreate real test-day conditions:

  • Use a laptop or approved device.
  • Work in a quiet space with minimal interruptions.
  • Complete the test in one sitting, including the scheduled break.
  • Practice with the calculator setup your student plans to use on test day.
  • Review answers only after the test is complete.

The Math section permits calculator use throughout, and Bluebook includes a built-in Desmos graphing calculator. Students should practice through the tools they expect to use ahead of time so they can use them efficiently on test day.

When the test is finished, don’t look at the score alone; assess it holistically. Review whether your student is finishing each section on time. See whether certain question types still cause trouble, and whether their confidence is improving.

Those details supply a much clearer picture of readiness.

Prepare With Purpose

A strong SAT plan is built on deliberate practice: diagnose, target, review, and repeat. Homeschool families can tailor preparation to the individual student instead of forcing every learner into the same schedule.

Combined with consistent skill practice and realistic digital test simulations, that support can help your student approach the SAT feeling prepared, focused, and confident.
